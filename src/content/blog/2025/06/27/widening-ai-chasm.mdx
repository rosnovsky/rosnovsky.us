I've been following advancements in generative AI/LLMs with mixed feelings. On the one hand, I love technology (and I don't mean "you can now enrich our shareholders faster" kind of technologies, but rather "folk's lives will improve" kind). On the other hand, it's irresponsible, short-sighted, and dangerous to ignore the potential for "AI" to exacerbate existing inequities, and create new ones caused by "AI" itself.

> Someday I'll find energy to write about it in detail, but this day is not the day.

I've been laid off over 6 months ago, in part due to the company "embracing AI". Setting the "laid off" part aside, this decision to pivot is as uninspired and incompetent as it is emblematic of the times: executives who don't understand shit about "AI" (among many, many other things) wreck havoc on their employees, and more broadly, on the society.

So, "AI" creates more permanently and long-term unemployed people. People whose industries crumble under the weight of good-enough "AI" slop. People whose decades of acquiring skills became irrelevant and obsolete within weeks and months, on a dime.

These unemployed people are struggling to find new jobs because of "AI":

- "AI" processes job applications, and if your application doesn't satisfy the Mechanical Turk, you're shit out of luck.
- "AI" allows people to apply to hundreds of jobs per day. These applications are often irrelevant to the job descriptions, but they produce such a firehose of applications that the only way to deal with it is to throw them at "AI" and hope for the best.
- in order to use "AI", unemployed folks (virtually) _must_ pay for "AI services", which they can't afford, duh.
- there are entire industries that shrank by hundreds of thousands of jobs in the past year alone, in part due to "pivots" to "AI"
- IT unemployment in the U.S. hit 5.7% in February, more than a third higher than the national average. [[ref](https://technical.ly/professional-development/tech-job-market-trends-downturn/)]
- tech layoffs are rampant, with over half a million workers being sacked in the past couple of years

> We are here: "AI" causes permanent jobs loss, you need "AI" in order to find new job, but "AI" makes it virtually impossible, thanks to "AI" itself.

As an engineer, I now _must_ use "AI" not only to find a job, but also to stay relevant: the space is evolving so quickly that missing a few weeks of learning or updates can be fatal to any hopes of getting a semblance of a career ever again.

Setting aside the fight against hiring "AIs", just staying in the loop is expensive:

- want to learn some in-depth stuff? **paid courses**
- want to try or use a coding co-pilot? **paid copilot services**
- want to stay relevant while "AI" generators like `bolt.new` and `v0.dev` exist? **paid generative services**
- want to connect with others in the space? **paid conferences**

All of the above is expensive, getting more expensive to use over time (paradoxically, but look it up, there's a bunch of analysis around it), is prohibitively expensive to those unemployed, like yours truly and hundreds of thousands of others.

So here we are, locked in unemployment limbo without any good options, and it's not going to get any better until it gets hilariously much worse first.
